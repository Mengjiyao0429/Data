## 正文
I’m building a tool that generates new mathematics exam problems using an internal database of past problems. My current setup uses a RAG pipeline, Pinecone as the vector database, and GPT-5 as the LLM. Goal Produce fully original, coherent, mathematically valid problems that follow the style and difficulty of my dataset, without simply rephrasing or slightly modifying existing questions. Issue Even with RAG, the outputs are often incoherent. I frequently get: mathematically incorrect or unsolvable problems mixed or stitched together parts of unrelated questions shallow paraphrasing of existing items loss of structure and pedagogical logic It seems the model retrieves chunks from Pinecone and mechanically combines them rather than understanding the underlying style or patterns of the tasks. Constraint I cannot train a custom model, so I need to rely on RAG improvements, retrieval strategies, or prompt engineering. My questions How can I design a RAG pipeline that generates truly new math problems instead of incoherent mixtures of retrieved material? What best practices in chunking, retrieval strategy, prompt structure, or metadata can help avoid the “stitched chunks” effect? How can I enforce originality while keeping the style and structure of the problems in my dataset? Should I use a hybrid RAG approach, such as pattern retrieval, templates, scaffolding, multi step prompts, or structural retrieval, instead of classic semantic RAG? Are there better models than GPT-5 for generating mathematically coherent problems, such as LLaMA 3, Qwen, DeepSeek, or Mistral?
