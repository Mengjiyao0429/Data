## 正文
Background: I have developed and maintain a relatively large application using VB.net that does modeling, structural analysis, structural engineering, etc. One of the key steps during the analysis part is the assembly and solution of a large matrix (stiffness matrix). The matrix solver is in a DLL written in Fortran. The main VB.net code calls the DLL when needed and passes it a series of arrays. It works fine and is very fast. The only problem is that it is a bit awkward to maintain the large Fortran DLL, and might become even more difficult in the future. A few years ago, I rewrote the entire matrix solver in VB.net but kept it as a DLL. The logic and math were identical to the Fortran version, the only difference was the language. The VB.net DLL gave identical results to the Fortran DLL and executed at about the same speed on small models. But on big models, the VB.net DLL became very slow, so slow that it was unusable. It is hard to believe that the math computations would be so much slower. I gave up on it, got frustrated and threw it away. Now a few years later I am wondering if the slowdown with large models was not so much due to VB.net but because it was in a DLL executing over and over and passing large arrays back and forth. Question: If I simply incorporate the matrix solver into the main VB.net program as series of subroutines, 43 routines to be exact, would the speed problem go away ? Maybe there is more overhead interfacing with a VB.net DLL compared to a Fortran DLL ? There are many other computationally intensive things that are done in the main VB.net code, like transformations, graphic calculations, etc. that work just fine and execute very fast. But they are all contained within the body of the main program. Thank you for your help and any suggestions.
