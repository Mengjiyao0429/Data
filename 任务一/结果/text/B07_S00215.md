One absolutely enormous advantage new entrants have in the AI space is that they can start on third base. Anthropic had to spend billions and billions of dollars to get to Opus 4.6. A new entrant could distill its way into a model roughly on par with that far more cheaply, and spend its VC cash on things like marketing and price cutting rather than burning it on actually creating a good model. The existing players need to service mountains of debt and are locked into deals for compute based on outdated assumptions about efficiency. I'm not saying it would be EASY for a new entrant to waltz in, especially to the enterprise market. But it's not impossible especially on the consumer side: Meta was essentially a non-factor until Muse came out, and now most people think of Muse when they think of an AI agent (as opposed to Claude, which is just a chatbot in the public imagination, because that's what it was when it became popular).
