# Technology only way out for developing Nations

- 链接：https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=3153218
- 发布时间：2011-10-25
- 热度：点赞3

## 正文
I want to start a technology company to do research and churn out innovative ways to do stuff in Africa. Technology is the only way to our never-ending issues. It occurs to most of us in developing nations and should be obvious that adoption of best practices and especially automation technology is the only way out of poverty, perpetual famine, name it! Easier said than done, the key challenge is getting people to actually embrace said technology. I'm from Kenya, and not too long ago someone introduced a tea-picking machine to boost tea production, a high currency earner for Kenya! But the opposition that was faced was serious! This is the story of the introduction of computers all over again: People saw computers as a threat rather than a tool to boost productivity... One way to adopt a forgiving culture for technology is to involve would be consumers in the creative process of the technology in question. This will result in total ownership for the technology, and eventual adoption by others! I have a few projects I've been hoping to have adopted here in Kenya, and my challenge has been as described above. What I've been trying to do is rally support, by creating promotions and competitions to get people involved and thereby adopt said ideas. It isn't always easy, but its fun! One idea pertains to use of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles in conservation and other efforts (nairobi-uav.blogspot.com). Buying a UAV and flying in Nairobi is outrageous under the current model (where anything technological is perceived as the enemy), so I thought building one and getting Kenyans to own it would be easier, and so far I've gotten two or three people interested. I'm looking to have a competition to get people involved in a challenge to build a UAV, and win cool stuff! That will also give me an insight into who would be a potential go-to guy when I start-up. Another pertains to agriculture. We always have famine when the rains fail, and are therefore totally reliant on nature to determine our future, instead of us taking measures to ensure constant food supplies. So I decided to make a farm automation platform with pluggable farm implements, the subject of which is the basis for my research at Warwick University ( ) Ultimate, there are people here in Kenya with interesting ideas, and I hope to work with them to dream up new ones for the future!
