Hi HN, I spent the last few months talking to many agencies and startup teams about their AI adoption struggles. I built TinTekka to solve what I found, but the more interesting part is the patterns I discovered. The core insight: most AI tools fail in production not because they're bad, but because teams don't know how to integrate them into existing workflows. There's a massive gap between "this tool exists" and "this tool actually saves us time/money." What I built: - A questionnaire that maps team workflows, bottlenecks, and current tooling - A matching algorithm that recommends AI tools (not just popularity-based, but fit-based) - An implementation roadmap showing concrete steps to integrate each tool - ROI projections so teams know what to expect Why I'm sharing this on HN: The technical part I'd love feedback on is the workflow-to-tool matching logic. Now I'm prompting an LLM to yield the result; I tried to be thorough in Prompt Engineering. But I was wondering if rule-based scoring that weighs factors like team size, use case, existing stack compatibility, and integration difficulty would be a better approach or not. I'm curious if HN has seen this problem solved elsewhere, or if there are better approaches to representing "workflow fit" as a scoring problem. Also, I'm opening a partner network for AI tool builders. If you've built an AI tool and want direct introductions to teams actively looking for solutions (vs. cold outreach), that's the angle I'm exploring. Please try it (completely free, no credit card) Genuinely curious what I'm missing about how teams actually adopt AI tools, and whether this approach to matching is sound. Thanks, Faisal
