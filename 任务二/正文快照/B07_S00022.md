Regarding proof automation, this is actually a double-edged sword. I can build systems today that will perform various optimization techniques to try to get further into a proof via tactic search, and will eventually converge on a solution. However, the tactics will be a mess, much like the 1600 pages of tactics in the OpenAI NS proof. A good proof engineer builds specifications and proofs that other humans can read and understand. Even the tactics are organized around what can be explained, so that these can be hand checked. If you're building proofs of software, you want auditors to be able to look at these as well as the specification to see if you are missing something, such as a nonce re-use policy.
