# Ask HN: Maintenance/support heavy teams?

- 来源：Hacker News
- 链接：https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=8119792
- 发布时间：2014-08-01
- 热度：点赞1、评论2

## 正文
I manage a development team comprised of the following: 1 data arch/dev, 2 biz/front-end .NET devs, and two BAs. We use VSO for our SCM, product backlog, work items, etc. We have segregated environments that follow our SDLC: Dev, Test, UAT (staging), and Prod. An understandable reaction might be that the team composition I describe is a bit atypical/not ideal. I'm working towards building the team to have clearer separation of concerns as well as bringing on a full-time Software Test Analyst. Team responsibilities: - Supporting over 200 customers: this ranges from various levels of data ETL complexity, to customized development change requests to our internal systems and/or integration projects between our systems and theirs. In many cases, we become an adopted dev team since a given customer may be so small that they have no dev resources. Or, some larger customers say they'll have to wait years to get X project done through their internal IT, so they turn to us to get it done in weeks or months. - Supporting our internal departments: This is Help Desk tickets, internal Change Requests,CAPA responses, etc. 30% of my team's time is dedicated to this internal support on a weekly basis. - New development: we have a large product backlog. Shiny object syndrome is real and something I continue to reduce through education, metrics, etc. Dev efforts that need to be dedicated to our internal operations department are neglected for months on end because of some new customer project, partner alliance, etc. It's this last bit I struggle with finding ways to improve upon (deadlines, higher quality, clearer requirements). In Oct 2013 we moved away from a waterfall dev methodology to Agile. In a nutshell, I learned that Agile really doesn't fit well on teams that have a lot of maintenance/support. I've looked into Lean, Kanban, etc but am hesitant to switch to something else without hearing from the community about similar challenges and approaches.
