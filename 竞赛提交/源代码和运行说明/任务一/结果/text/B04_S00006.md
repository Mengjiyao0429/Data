# Amodei Says AI Will Cure Cancer in a Decade. Doctors Are Skeptical

- 发布时间：2026-11-13

## 正文
One of my core pieces of advice to AI leaders who get interested in biology is "do not say you will cure cancer, the medical establishment will immediately write you off as clueless". Instead, I recommend AI people say "we believe our new technology will be able to rapidly make significant improvements to the detection and treatment of cancer. We will work closely with medical researchers to help them use our tools effectively." When I did my own cancer research ~25 years ago, the only treatments were basically chemo or radio therapy. The outcomes were often terrible- even for people who lived, they suffered terribly. We are now in a state where new technologies are making absolutely amazing improvements in treatment- with far fewer side effects, which greatly increases quality of life in people undergoing the treatment, and after. I think if we keep grinding on this problem (as well as with many other diseases), we could very well make most cancers a non-death sentence that can be treated routinely with minimal side effects. Here is a thing that did not exist when I was a PhD student: a person with cancer goes to the doctor, they take a biopsy of the tumor and send it to a lab, which sequences the cancer genome. That genome is then input to an AI which understands a great deal about cancer immunology, and spits out a custom antibody which is then injected back into the person, and that antibody teaches the person's immune system to fight the cancer more effectively. There literally is an AI model (my coworkers developed one that is in clinical trials now) integrated in the treatment process, which is absolutely necessary for the high effectivity of the treatment. That knowledge alone inspires me to do my best work, with the belief that it will have positive outcomes. There's another open question- to what extent do we need to understand the underlying biology of cancer, versus having models that integrate enormous numbers of variables effectively to come up with treatments without a mechanistic model. It's quite clear we really do not understand how drugs work (no matter how much publications claim we do), and probably by improving methods by which we can sample the enormous space of potential drugs/treatments. And finally: how can we made this affordable? Cancer treatment in the US is obscenely expensive with many levels of rent seekers who constantly add unnecessary cost to treatment. That will likely require aggressive and intelligent legal regulation (clearly the market is not efficient), and I don't see any sort of political will to make it happen.
