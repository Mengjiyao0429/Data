# Show HN: AI Red Lines – write down what would make you act on AI

- 来源：Hacker News
- 链接：https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50472907
- 发布时间：2026-11-12

## 正文
So I have my own version of what you are talking about that I've been building up that's about the levels of AI usage: Level 0 - No AI - what I'd recommend if you are ethically against AI Level 1 - AI in search engines (a better Stack Overflow) - what I'd recommend if you are trying to build ideas on your own and don't want AI influencing you Level 2 - AI with long discussions (such as going to chatGPT or Gemini) - what I'd recommend if you don't want to give your IP to an AI Level 3 - AI agents reading your data but not writing it - what I'd recommend for most people concerned about AI, as it allows code reviews which prevent critical security bugs while you retain complete control Level 4 - AI agents writing data, humans designing (AKA short leash) - what I'd recommend for people wanting to dive into AI but retain control, code-wise this is like reviewing 100 line changes at a time Level 5 - AI agents writing data and design, humans reviewing (AKA long leash or reverse centaur) - what I'd recommend against for most scenarios. Code-wise this is reviewing 1000+ line changes at a time, which faces the hand-off issue where people will stop learning and just hit OK. The hand-off issue is what creates code-slop. Yet, it's what most companies are doing right now. I would only recommend it for particular large non-innovative tasks such as updating a dependency that is used across your codebase. Level 6 - Humans writing requirements, AI doing everything else (AKA Spec driven development) - what I'd recommend for anyone who wants to use AI to the fullest. However, IMO most companies do this wrong. You want a high-quality human built requirements document that is placed in a separate section that is read-only to AI. This means writing requirements will actually be slower compared to pre-AI, but after that, everything else is pretty much instant and you skip immediately to testing. Any issues in testing should result in updating requirements to match and regenerating the product. The problem I've seen is that most ordinary people have no idea of these levels and only think in binary terms, and it gets ugly. You may have seen the latest news about the developers of Big Walk using AI to fix a bug, which would either be level 2, 3, or 4 depending, and people are freaking out. Meanwhile I'd guess 90% of software companies are currently at level 5 or 6, so Big Walk would be very anti-AI relative to most games being built this year. Similarly, there are store fronts and competitions that require no AI, but they obviously aren't thinking about level 1, as no one would require developers to not use Google (or at least change default settings to turn off AI summaries).
