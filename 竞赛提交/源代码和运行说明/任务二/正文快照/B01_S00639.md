# Ask HN: How to avoid places that do not allow me to grow as a developer?

- 来源：Hacker News
- 链接：https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15612675
- 发布时间：2017-11-02
- 热度：点赞3、评论3

## 正文
So far I've been in a several companies and the story is always the same - the interview doesn't really raise any major red flags, the pay is decent, I take the offer and a month or two in I feel like leaving because there's very little or no opportunities to grow. The companies seem to always have most - of not all - of the flaws: - no Glassdoor reviews - bait and switch, hired for the knowledge of a newer or popular technology, yet work is 100% legacy systems - no mentoring of any sort - poor or no documentation and/or tests - raising the issue gets me murky promises of things being better in the future, but no actual improvements ever come - weird and dated tools and systems - verson control exists, but for some reason the projects I'm working on do not use it - lots of grunt work that should have been automated ages ago - suggestions of improvements either met with resistance, ridicule and excuses - people working here are not passionate about technology I'm looking for advice on how can I find a place where I can truly learn. I don't mind occasional grunt work or having to navigate through a legacy system, however with 2 years of experience of doing mostly that I feel like dedicating myself towards this type of work is actively hurting my future prospects. I just want a place where I can look back one month and say "in this month I learned [x]". So far nearly every single improvement came from learning on my own, after work, and it's getting to the point where this is the only source of improvement for my skills. I'm interested in what I can do to remedy the situation. The easy answer is probably "networking", however most of the time I'm on my own. How do I leave this broken circle of terrible jobs?
