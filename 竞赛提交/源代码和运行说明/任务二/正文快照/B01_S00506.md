When photography was first introduced in the early-to-mid 19th century, it was indeed seen as a threat to traditional art forms, particularly painting. The ability to capture realistic images quickly and accurately led some to believe that there would be no need for painters, whose skills in capturing reality on canvas could not compete with the precision and speed of photography. However, rather than spelling the end of painting, photography actually pushed the art form to evolve. Artists began to explore new styles and techniques, resulting in movements like Impressionism, Expressionism, and Abstract Art. These movements emphasized the personal, emotional, and subjective aspects of art, which could not be captured by a photograph. As a result, painting continued to thrive and develop, even as photography became more widespread and accessible. The concerns about AI and its potential impact on various professions, including programming, can be seen as somewhat parallel to the initial fears about photography's impact on art. In the case of programming, AI can certainly help automate some aspects of the process, making it more efficient and streamlined. However, the design, development, and implementation of AI systems still require human input, especially when it comes to understanding the needs of users and solving complex, real-world problems. Moreover, as AI technology advances, it may also open up new opportunities for human programmers to explore novel applications and innovative solutions. In conclusion, while new technologies can initially appear threatening to established professions, history has shown that they often lead to an evolution in those fields rather than their outright replacement.
