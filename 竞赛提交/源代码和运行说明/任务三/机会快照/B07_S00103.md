## 正文
So here's a version that doesn't glaze airlines: it's an extension of dynamic pricing, everyone knows that prices (generally) rise as the date of the flight approaches so that airlines can get more money out of business travelers and single moms going to funerals who have inelastic demand, while vacationers with their elastic demand aren't priced out. But then how do you maximize money from vacationers? Well, some are going to have more flexibility with dates than others and you want to get the most money out of them no matter what, so you don't show how your prices change day to day (both day of flight and day of booking). Then, people with more to spend will just book whenever and the aforementioned single mother will waste hours checking various combinations daily to find a price she can afford. That's the customer that United deems "worth it", they won't book for any price higher than $50 so they add a ton of friction to force that customer to put effort into finding that price. But if anyone can overcome that friction with an AI agent, airlines lose out on that dynamic pricing signal.
